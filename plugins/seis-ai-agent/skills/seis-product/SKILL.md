# SEIS Product

Use this skill when SEIS work needs product clarity before or alongside
implementation: what should exist, who it serves, how it is validated, and what
should not be built yet.

## Workflow

1. Define the user, job, product surface, and expected outcome.
2. Convert broad intent into a bounded slice with acceptance criteria.
3. Map the slice to SEIS lanes: governance, cloud, code, design, data, security,
   automation, research, and docs.
4. Identify release blockers, rollback requirements, and non-goals.
5. Prefer real product surfaces over marketing-only artifacts.
6. Keep Apple-first, AI-native, open-source, accessible, and maintainable
   direction visible without overbuilding.
7. Tie product claims to validation commands, screenshots, tests, docs, or
   generated reports.

## Product Slice Template

Use this shape for substantial product planning:

- outcome
- target user
- primary workflow
- acceptance criteria
- affected surfaces
- validation evidence
- rollout and rollback
- risks and non-goals
- next implementation step

## Guardrails

- Do not turn vague ideas into broad rewrites.
- Do not claim release readiness without validation and CI evidence.
- Do not create a landing page when the requested work needs a usable app,
  tool, dashboard, or workflow surface.
- Keep roadmap decisions source-controlled when they affect long-term SEIS
  direction.
